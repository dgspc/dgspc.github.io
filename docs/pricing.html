The awkward ones

Questions we'd rather answer here.

Including the one about whether this model quietly punishes you for building well.

Can we run every agent on Observe and never pay?

Yes, and the agent count is unlimited while you do. You'd have full traces, replay and cost accounting across the whole estate for nothing.

What you wouldn't have is anything that stops an action, any risk scoring, or alerts reaching your on-call. Teams usually run that way for a quarter, then find one thing they wish had been blocked.

What happens to traces older than 30 days on the free tier?

Nothing disappears. Full step-level replay covers 30 days; after that you keep 12 months of metadata — what ran, when, which tools, which systems, what it cost.

So you can still find the day an agent touched something it shouldn't have. Opening that run and watching it back is what needs a paid plan. We'd rather you hit a locked door than an empty shelf.

Is there a limit on Observe volume?

A fair-use ceiling rather than a wall. Past it we sample the repetitive traffic — repeated reads, unchanged queries — and never sample anything above baseline or touching a policy-relevant path.

A supervision tool that stops recording mid-incident is worthless, so the ceiling degrades rather than cuts off, and it starts a conversation instead of a bill.

What exactly counts as one billable agent?

A distinct identity, holding its own credentials, with at least one enforcement rule pointed at it. Ten instances of the same agent behind a queue is one agent. Two agents sharing a codebase but holding different access is two.

Traced-only agents never count, however many there are. We count what your security team would count, because they're the ones who have to review it.

Doesn't per-agent pricing push us to build fewer, bigger agents?

It would, and that's the honest weakness of the model. Cramming six jobs into one agent to save $59 a month is a bad architecture decision that our pricing would have caused.

So two things. Agents under 10,000 actions a month don't count toward your total, which covers most of the small specialised ones. And past 100 agents the per-agent rate falls faster than the count rises. If you still find the model bending your design, tell us — that's a pricing bug on our side.

What happens if we stop paying?

Enforcement stops. Your agents keep running — we won't hold your production hostage over an invoice. You get 90 days of full console access, then a final export of the complete trail.

The hash chain stays independently verifiable afterwards, with or without us.
