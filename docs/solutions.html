One record. Three very different questions.

Security wants to know what got touched. Operations wants to know whether the work got done. Compliance wants to know who said yes. Most organisations answer those from three systems that disagree. Spectre answers all three from one.

By role

What each team actually gets.

Same deployment, same trail, three sets of defaults. Nobody has to learn another team's tooling to answer their own question.

01
Security

Treat every agent as an identity with standing access

Because that is what it is. An agent holds credentials, reaches into production, and acts continuously without anyone approving each move. Spectre gives it the same scrutiny you would give a contractor with a permanent badge.

What you get

  • Risk score per agent, recalculated each run
  • Data-movement rules enforced before egress
  • Session replay on anything held or blocked
  • Alerts into your existing SIEM and on-call rota

What it replaces

  • Log-scraping to reconstruct what an agent did
  • Quarterly access reviews that miss machine identities
  • Trusting a framework's own telemetry to police itself
02
Operations

Find out whether the handover actually worked

You gave a workflow to an agent to get time back. Spectre tells you whether you did — completion rate, cost per finished task, where runs stall or hand back to a human, measured against the manual baseline you started from.

What you get

  • Success rate and cost per completed task
  • Where runs stall, retry or bounce back to a person
  • Queue depth and time-to-finish by workflow
  • Side-by-side against the pre-agent baseline

What it replaces

  • Guessing at ROI from token spend alone
  • Dashboards that show model latency but not business outcome
  • Finding out a workflow broke when a customer complains
03
Compliance and legal

Answer the question months after anyone remembers

The hard part of an agent incident is never the day it happens. It is the review eleven months later, when the engineer has left and the logs have rotated. Spectre keeps the answer where an auditor can reach it without a forensic project.

What you get

  • Append-only trail with the approver on record
  • Evidence packs by date, agent or single decision
  • Retention and residency you set per workspace
  • Reviewer sign-off built into the flow, not bolted on

What it replaces

  • Screenshot folders and a shared spreadsheet
  • Asking engineering to reconstruct a decision from memory
  • Discovering your retention policy never covered agent logs
