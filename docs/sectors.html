In detail

What we see in each.

Drawn from deployments rather than desk research. Where we've only seen one or two examples, we say so rather than generalising from them.

01
Financial services

The accountable human has to be nameable

Most financial regulation was written assuming a person made the decision. An agent doesn't remove that expectation, it just makes it harder to evidence — which is the entire problem Spectre exists to solve.

Operational resilience rules also pull third-party dependencies into scope, so the enforcement path being self-hostable tends to matter more here than anywhere else.

Agents we see

  • KYC and onboarding document review
  • Reconciliation and exception handling
  • Client reporting and renewal operations
  • First-pass triage on surveillance alerts

Where holds fire

  • Client data crossing the account boundary
  • Anything that could initiate a payment
  • Bulk export ahead of a reporting deadline
  • Off-hours writes to books and records
02
Healthcare

Every access to a record is a question you'll be asked

The standard is minimum necessary access, evidenced per access rather than per policy. An agent reading a thousand records to summarise one is a compliance problem even when nothing leaves the building.

Healthcare also carries the highest breach cost of any sector, which changes how a board weighs the cost of supervision against the cost of not having it.

Agents we see

  • Prior authorisation and claims preparation
  • Clinical documentation drafting
  • Coding and billing review
  • Patient correspondence triage

Where holds fire

  • Identifiers in outbound communication
  • Record reads far above the task's need
  • Anything reaching a non-covered system
  • Retention windows about to be breached
03
Public sector

Records obligations that predate autonomous software

Records management and transparency duties were written for filing cabinets and still apply. They don't have an exemption for a decision an agent made at three in the morning, and freedom-of-information requests reach agent activity like anything else.

Procurement scrutiny of automated decision-making is tightening across most jurisdictions, so being able to show the decision path matters as much as the outcome.

Agents we see

  • Case triage and routing
  • Information request response drafting
  • Records classification and retention
  • Correspondence handling at volume

Where holds fire

  • Decisions affecting an individual, unreviewed
  • Records disposal without sign-off
  • Actions with no named accountable officer
  • Anything leaving sovereign infrastructure
04
Technology

Your regulator is your customers' auditors

The pressure here rarely comes from a regulator. It arrives as a security questionnaire from a large customer asking what your agents can reach in the environment holding their data — and that question is getting more specific every quarter.

This sector has the fastest agent adoption, the deepest production access, and usually the least governance around either. It's where we see the most surprising first month.

Agents we see

  • Code review and dependency triage
  • Incident response and on-call assistance
  • Customer support at first line
  • Data pipeline and infrastructure operations

Where holds fire

  • Writes to production without review
  • Credential and secret access
  • Customer data pulled into development
  • Infrastructure changes outside a window
